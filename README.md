# Mailern

A native SwiftUI email workflow prototype for iPhone, with fictional mail across editable accounts. Requires iOS 18 or later and Xcode 26 or later.

Open `Mailern.xcodeproj`, select the Mailern scheme and your device, then choose your development team under Signing & Capabilities and run. No packages or provider credentials are needed.

## Try the workflow

- Each mailbox has a Filter toggle in the top toolbar. When active, the toggle stays on the right and its capsule expands to the left with “Filtered by” and the current criteria. Tap the criteria or its arrow to open Filters. The button toggles filtering without clearing its settings. Choose any combination of accounts, All/Unread/Read in Inbox and Snoozed, Only drafts in Inbox and Snoozed, or Only sent in Settled. Conditions combine with AND; selected accounts combine with OR. Sent matches conversations containing an outgoing message. Settings persist separately for each mailbox. Search and Select All use the filtered results; the Inbox badge still counts all unread conversations. Inbox keeps pinned items first, Snoozed orders by return time, and Settled keeps newest first.
- Each row shows a sender avatar, everyone who wrote in the conversation, a message count for threads, the subject, one line of the latest message, and the account name with its color. Your own messages show as Me; sent-only conversations show their recipients.
- Opening a conversation marks it read and keeps it in its current section. The latest message is open and uses the full width of the screen. Folded messages sit together in a rounded group with their sender, date, and first line; tap one to open it, or tap an open message's sender to fold it. Conversations with more than four messages show the first message and the last two, with one row for the messages between them. Under an open message's sender, tap "to me" for the full addresses and date. Quoted history at the end of a reply folds behind a "…" button. Mailern only folds a quote it can prove: an "On … wrote:" line followed by exactly an earlier message in the conversation, or a trailing run of lines that start with `>`. Text written below or between quoted lines always shows.
- Swipe left for Settle or Snooze. In Settled, swipe left to restore.
- Swipe right for Read/Unread and Pin. A full right swipe toggles read status outside Settled.
- Hold a conversation for a compact, scrollable preview with the tools visible. Drag the tools downward to enlarge the card; drag upward to shrink it and reveal the menu again. Tap a reply shortcut or menu action. The preview shows the latest message first; scroll down to read the earlier messages, newest to oldest. Reading the preview preserves read status; tapping it opens the conversation, where earlier messages can be opened.
- Use the thread's Reply menu for Reply, Reply All, and Forward of the latest message. Each open message has its own reply button and a menu with the same three actions, which quote and address that message. A reply started from a message keeps its own draft, separate from the toolbar's reply draft. Sending a reply adds a message and settles that conversation. New messages and forwards appear in Settled. Sent mail waits 10 seconds before it goes out: tap Undo on the Sending banner to reopen it in the composer. Settings → Undo send sets the delay to Off, 5, 10, or 20 seconds.
- Use More → Demo at the top left to receive new mail, receive junk mail, receive a reply to any conversation, or reset all demo data.
- Snooze for one or three hours, this evening, tomorrow, next week, or a custom date. Options are sorted by return time; past evening times are hidden. Next week defaults to Monday at 09:00. Use Snooze settings in More or the snooze picker to change the presets. Settings persist across launches and only affect future snoozes. Expired snoozes return while the app is active, or when it next opens. The prototype does not run a background mail service or send notifications.
- Hold Send in the composer for Send Later and Remind Me If No Reply. Both use the snooze presets or a custom date. Scheduled mail is listed under Mailboxes → Scheduled and as a card in its thread, with Edit, Send Now, and Cancel Send. Edit and Cancel Send return it to Drafts. Scheduled mail is sent while the app is active, or when it next opens. A conversation settles when its reply is sent, not when it is scheduled.
- With a reminder, sent mail waits in Snoozed until the reminder time. A reply ends the reminder. When the time passes without one, the conversation returns to Inbox marked No reply. Moving the conversation by hand, including to Trash or Junk, also ends the reminder. Undo restores it.
- Mute a conversation from the thread's More actions. Replies to a muted conversation join it without moving it or marking it unread, and muted rows show a bell icon. A reply that answers a reminder on a muted conversation settles it quietly.
- Incoming replies immediately return a conversation to Inbox as unread, including from Snoozed and Settled, unless it is muted. Settling marks conversations read. Settled conversations cannot be marked unread. Pins remain independent of workflow status.
- The Undo card disappears after five seconds. Each new action restarts that window. Undo reverses the most recent workflow move, including the prior read status when undoing Settle. Incoming mail, sending, and attaching a reply draft to a pending workflow move invalidate Undo so it cannot hide a newer message.
- Search covers subjects, senders, accounts, and message bodies. Queries of three or more characters use a full-text index, so any part of a word matches. Under the search field, This section searches the open mailbox and All mail searches Inbox, Snoozed, and Settled together, in sections that name each result's mailbox, newest first within each. All mail leaves out Junk and Trash and ignores filters, and bulk selection is unavailable. Closing search returns to This section.
- Settings → After settling chooses what a thread shows after Settle: Return to list (the default) or Open next conversation. The next conversation is the one below in the list you opened the thread from, including its search and filter, or the one above when it was the last. Back returns to the list.
- Settle, Snooze, Pin, Read/Unread, Move to Trash or Junk, and Restore play a light haptic, wherever you start them. Opening a conversation and incoming mail do not.
- Settings → Accounts lists the accounts. Tap one to change its name, address, color, or signature, or to delete it. Deleting an account removes its conversations and drafts after a confirmation; the last account cannot be deleted. Add Account creates another fictional account. New messages are sent from the first account.
- New messages, replies, and forwards start with the sending account's signature, above any quote. Changing From in a new message or forward swaps the signature, unless you edited it.
- Scheduled, Junk, and Trash are under Mailboxes in the More menu at the top left, not in the tab bar. Each opens on the current tab's stack. Move a conversation to Trash from the thread toolbar, the peek menu, bulk selection, or a Trash swipe action configured in Settings. Move it to Junk from the thread's More actions or bulk Mark. Conversations keep their workflow status while in Junk or Trash, so Restore and Not Junk return them to the section they left. Undo reverses the move. Conversations with reply drafts ask to discard the drafts first. Scheduled replies stay scheduled, but deleting a conversation permanently also discards them.
- Moving to Junk blocks every other sender in the conversation. Later mail from a blocked address goes to Junk, and the block survives deleting that mail. Not Junk unblocks those senders. An incoming reply returns a conversation from Trash to Inbox, but a reply in Junk stays there.
- Swipe left in Junk or Trash to restore or delete permanently. Select supports both actions in bulk. Empty Trash and permanent deletion ask for confirmation and cannot be undone. Conversations are deleted automatically 30 days after they move to Trash, while the app is active or when it next opens.

Messages, workflow state, accounts, and blocked senders are saved in a SQLite database in the app's Application Support directory, with one row per conversation, so a mailbox action rewrites only the conversations it changed. A trigram full-text index in the same database covers search. Drafts and scheduled mail are saved together as atomic JSON. Each sent message keeps its draft's ID, so if the draft cannot be removed after sending, the next attempt removes it without sending a second copy. Scheduled mail that cannot be sent returns to Drafts with an alert. Attachment bytes live in separate files, so neither store rewrites them. Loads map attachment files on demand, and unreferenced files are removed when storage is updated. Draft JSON encoding and writes run in an ordered background queue, and dismissal waits for successful persistence. The composer uses native sheet transitions, recipient and sender fields, and a rich text editor. Use Aa for text styles, fonts, color, lists, alignment, and indentation. Formatting participates in native Undo and Redo. Formatting uses Apple's native panel. The paperclip imports files or photos, with a 25 MB total limit and native file previews. Save Draft and Delete Draft are in the close button's menu. Swiping the sheet away saves an edited draft before dismissal. If saving fails, the editor stays open with its contents intact. Unreadable draft storage is protected from overwrite; the Drafts storage alert offers Retry. Drafts have an orange row tint and a composer icon. Reply drafts stay with their existing conversation in its current mailbox; new messages and forwards have separate rows in Inbox until snoozed. Open the row to read the draft at the bottom, then use Edit draft or Discard draft. Standalone drafts support Pin and Snooze through the configured swipe actions and their reader. Their Settle action asks to discard the draft or cancel. Conversations with drafts retain Pin, Snooze, Restore, and Read/Unread outside Settled. Settling a conversation with drafts asks to discard those drafts or cancel, including in bulk selection. Failed draft writes preserve the drafts and prevent settling. Snoozed drafts return to Inbox when due. Draft text and recipients are included in search. If storage remains unavailable after dismissing the alert, use More → Retry loading drafts. Replies include an editable quote and settle the source conversation when sent; forwards create a separate settled conversation. All sending remains simulated. The reader displays local text, rich outgoing messages, HTML mail, and attachments. HTML mail runs in a web view with JavaScript off and no stored cookies. Remote images, styles, and fonts stay blocked until you tap Load images, because senders use them to see when mail is opened. Embedded `data:` images always show. Frames and redirects never load. Mail laid out for a fixed width is scaled to fit. HTML mail spans the full width of the screen, with the page's own margins. Mail that styles itself for dark mode follows the system appearance; other mail keeps a white page in dark mode. The demo Sample Weekly newsletter shows the light-only case with a blocked tracking pixel, and the snoozed workshop message shows the dark-aware case. Web links, email addresses, and phone numbers in messages are tappable. A `mailto:` link opens a new message with its recipients, subject, and body; other links open in the system's default app, such as the browser for web links. Plain-text mail shows as written and is not read as Markdown.

Drag the composer content to hide the keyboard without closing the message. Tap any field to continue editing.

Tap Add recipient beside To to browse known senders. Search recipients by name or email address, then tap a result to add it to To.

## Code

`Mailern/Core` contains conversation models, demo messages, and the observable store. `ConversationPersistence` separates storage from state changes; `SQLitePersistence` uses the SQLite library that ships with iOS, so no packages are needed. `Mailern/Views` contains the native SwiftUI screens. Messages carry Message-ID, In-Reply-To, and References headers, and incoming mail joins the conversation in the same account that holds the message it answers. Workflow status is local. When providers are added, Settle maps to archive (Gmail removes the Inbox label; IMAP moves mail to the Archive folder), and Snooze stays on the device, so snoozed mail remains in the provider's inbox. No provider integrations or backend are included.

Run `scripts/check-workflow.sh` for isolated state and persistence checks. It creates a unique temporary data file, separate from the app's demo data.

Prototype refinements use patch versions. Increment the build number for each distributed build; reserve minor versions for larger milestones.

Run `scripts/check-ui.sh DISPOSABLE_SIMULATOR_ID` for the full suite. It builds once and uses four additional disposable Simulator workers plus the supplied fixture Simulator for duration-balanced batches. The two native picker tests stay on the fixture Simulator, which also runs ordinary cases so it does not sit idle after imports. After worker creation or cold boots, the picker tests finish first, then at most three worker batches run at a time. This limits startup contention while the workers become ready for later runs. The first run creates the workers. Later runs reuse them to avoid repeated Simulator setup. Workers stay booted between runs to keep reruns fast. Run `scripts/check-ui.sh DISPOSABLE_SIMULATOR_ID --stop` to release their running resources. They remain in the Simulator device list; delete them there when no longer needed. The script reports the total time, including build and fixture setup, and merges every batch into one result bundle. Pass an additional-worker count from 1 to 10 as the second argument. Use fewer workers on machines with less memory. Stop the pool before reducing its worker count. Each UI test has its own class so the runner can balance individual tests using recorded durations. Each test uses separate mail, draft, attachment, and preference storage; relaunches within a test preserve that storage. In script runs, DEBUG fixture plumbing resets stores, preferences, navigation, and sheets between independent cases in the same app process. A request/session acknowledgment verifies each reset. Every later launch within a case still restarts the actual process and checks that its process identity changed. Without the script's control environment, tests use normal XCTest launches. The long-thread test seeds its history directly, then drives a real incoming reply. Each batch checks that every expected case ran exactly once, with no failures or skips, before the merged result is validated. The script disables OS activity logs in the test app to reduce Simulator logging overhead. XCTest activities and failure screenshots provide test diagnostics. Automatic screenshot attachments are disabled. Issues recorded on the main thread attach the current screen directly to the issue, so failure screenshots are included in `xcresulttool export attachments --only-failures`. Off-main-thread issues retain their original diagnostics without a custom screenshot. Named captures and screenshot assertions still run; successful named attachments are discarded. Product → Test in Xcode also permits parallel execution, but native picker tests require the fixture setup from the script. For the composer suite, run `scripts/check-composer-ui.sh SIMULATOR_ID` on a booted disposable Simulator. The script installs the app, copies the fictional file fixture into its Documents directory, imports a sample image into Photos, and tests file and photo import, formatting, draft recovery, reply/forward sending, and swipe dismissal, including forced storage failures. Native editor tests check mixed formatting and rich-text persistence.

The checked-in Xcode project works directly. After adding source files, regenerate it with `python3 scripts/create-project.py`. Edit `Mailern/AppIcon.icon` in Icon Composer. Its three vector groups separate the rounded upper wing, rear wing, and body, with the upper wing in front. Xcode compiles the layered icon and its appearance variants directly from this file. Building the icon requires Xcode 26 or later.

## Remote development installation

`Version.xcconfig` sets the app version and the local Xcode build default. Change the version there before a new release. Distributed build numbers come from `builds.json` on the repository's separate `build-registry` branch. That branch is shared by all machines, clones, and worktrees. Do not merge it into application branches or reset its history. `python3 scripts/builds.py list` shows the version, number, source commit, source branch, and result for each build. An asterisk after the commit means the build includes uncommitted changes. The first recorded build may have unknown source history when it was reported manually.

`scripts/package-demo.sh TEAM_ID` reserves the next build number on GitHub, then archives and exports a development-signed IPA using an existing Xcode signing account/profile. It requires the authenticated GitHub CLI with repository write access and a selected full Xcode installation. Each reservation uses GitHub's file SHA check, so competing builds must read the latest registry and retry instead of taking the same number. A failed or interrupted build keeps its number. A reservation left by an interrupted process remains `reserved`; it is not proof of a successful build. There is no offline counter. Use this command for every distributed build, including builds from PR branches. Older branches must include these scripts before packaging. A manual `xcodebuild archive` bypasses the registry.

Outputs go into `build/releases/NUMBER`, so parallel builds do not share archive, export, or DerivedData paths. Its `build.json` records the version, build number, source commit and branch, source digest, uncommitted state, and IPA checksum. The registry stores the same metadata and whether export succeeded. Exported does not mean published or installed. The archive fails if source files change while Xcode is building. Use an isolated worktree for each concurrent source change. Pass `--version 0.6.1` to choose an explicit version or `--expect-build 37` to stop before building if that number is no longer available. Build numbers increase across all versions and branches. The iPhone must be registered in the signing profile and have Developer Mode enabled. A development-signed app expires with its provisioning profile.

`scripts/serve-demo.py HTTPS_BASE_URL --build-record build/releases/NUMBER/build.json` creates an OTA manifest and a plain install page with light and dark themes in `build/install`. It reads provenance from the build record rather than the current checkout, checks the IPA checksum, and checks that the record, archive, and IPA agree on identity and version. It refuses to replace an IPA with different content under the same build number. Pass an optional output directory as the second argument to prepare a separate install page for a PR build. Use a different output directory and HTTPS path for each parallel install page; the default page shows the last build explicitly published there. The generator does not start a server. Serve only the installation directory over trusted HTTPS, such as a dedicated Tailscale Serve path. Open its page in Safari on the registered iPhone with Tailscale connected. The computer serving the files must remain awake and connected.

For a new repository, initialize the registry once with `python3 scripts/builds.py init --last-build NUMBER --version VERSION --note 'Last known distributed build; source unknown'`. Use the last number actually distributed across all machines. Initialization refuses to replace an existing registry. Do not initialize each clone separately.

Run `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s Tests -p 'test_builds.py'` to check concurrent number allocation, failure handling, and publication metadata without signing or changing the remote registry.

Settings in the More menu includes accounts, snooze preferences, swipe actions, the Undo Send delay, what to show after Settle, and Labs. Compose is at the bottom right by default while preserving native tabs. iOS 27 uses the system's prominent trailing tab; earlier versions use a native bottom toolbar. Labs can assign the Inbox pull-down gesture to Search or New email. This experiment is off by default and persists locally. Pull actions require iOS 18 or later. Start a pull while the Inbox is stationary at the very top. Pull farther to fill the ring; a soft tap and brief pulse indicate readiness. Move back up to cancel or pull down again to rearm before releasing. Search opens immediately on release. Compose uses the same native transition as its button.

Settings → Swipe actions configures Full Swipe, Additional Action 1, and Additional Action 2 separately for left and right swipes. Each slot is optional. With Full Swipe set to None, the additional actions remain available as buttons without triggering on a full swipe. Choose Settle / Restore, Read / Unread, Snooze, Pin / Unpin, Move to Trash, or None. On standalone drafts, Move to Trash asks to discard the draft. The same configuration applies in all workflow mailboxes, persists locally, and can be reset to the defaults. Junk and Trash keep their Restore and Delete Permanently actions.

Use Select at the top of any mailbox for bulk actions. Select All selects the current search results and changes to Deselect All when every result is selected. The bottom toolbar offers Settle or Restore, Snooze, Move to Trash, and a Mark menu with contextual Read/Unread, Pin/Unpin, and Move to Junk actions. Mixed selections offer Mark Read and Pin. Settled omits Read/Unread actions. In Snoozed, Mark also offers Restore to Inbox. Selection never marks mail read. Cancel exits selection; cancelling the snooze picker keeps the selection. Undo reverses the entire batch.
